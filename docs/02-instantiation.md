# Instantiation: From Abstract to Concrete

## The Problem

Given a core movement like "inspire someone trapped to leave an oppressive situation," there are infinite possible stories:
- A woman leaving an abusive marriage
- An ethnic group seceding to form their own nation
- An employee quitting a toxic workplace
- A cult member escaping
- A country breaking from colonial rule
- A protective family dog who must leave to save his family

How do we choose?

## Approaches (All Valid)

### 1. User Specifies Directly

The user may already know what they want:
> "Make it about an ethnic minority in a fantasy kingdom"

In this case, proceed directly to mapping the abstract elements to this concrete world.

### 2. Engine Suggests, User Chooses

When the user provides only the core movement or target audience, the engine should:

1. Generate 3-5 distinct concrete instantiations
2. For each, provide a **rough outline**—not just a label, but enough story shape to evaluate
3. Present these to the user via the question interface
4. User may:
   - Choose one
   - Ask for more detail on one before deciding
   - Request new options
   - Specify constraints to narrow the field

**Example presentation:**

> **Option A: The Secession**
> An ethnic minority in a declining empire. For generations, they've been "tolerated"—allowed to exist but never to thrive. The story follows a council member who has always advocated for patience and working within the system. When a new imperial policy threatens their language, she must choose between her lifelong strategy of accommodation and leading a movement she once opposed.

> **Option B: The Family Dog**
> A protective German Shepherd has spent his life guarding a family. But the family is slowly being destroyed from within—the father's alcoholism, the mother's denial, the children's fear. The dog understands threat, but this threat lives in the house. To protect them, he must do the unthinkable: leave, and in leaving, force them to see what he saw.

> **Option C: The Startup**
> A brilliant engineer at a tech company founded by her mentor. The company was once idealistic; now it's become everything they hated. She's been rationalizing for years. The story follows her through a product launch that will harm millions—and her choice to either blow the whistle or stay silent.

### 3. Target Audience Matching

When the user specifies a target audience:
> "I want women in abusive marriages to feel inspired to leave"

The engine should:

1. **Research current context**: What fiction is this audience consuming? What movies are being made for them? What real-world events are impacting their lives?

2. **Choose resonant but not literal**: Often, the most powerful stories are *not* directly about the audience's situation. A woman in an abusive marriage might be more moved by a story about an ethnic group's liberation than by a direct portrayal of domestic abuse. The metaphor allows her to see her situation from outside.

3. **Consider emotional safety**: Direct representation can be re-traumatizing. Metaphorical distance allows engagement without direct confrontation.

## The Mapping Process

Once a concrete instantiation is chosen, create explicit mappings:

| Abstract Element | Concrete Instantiation |
|-----------------|----------------------|
| Trapped entity | The ethnic minority / The woman / The employee |
| Oppressor | The empire / The husband / The company |
| The trap mechanism | Economic dependence / Emotional manipulation / Golden handcuffs |
| The difficult choice | Secession / Divorce / Resignation |
| What's at stake | Cultural survival / Safety / Career identity |
| The catalyst | New oppressive policy / Violence / Ethical violation |

This mapping table becomes the foundation for the next level of the tree, where we add characters, relationships, and plot structure.

## Key Principle

The instantiation is not just a "setting" or "skin" applied to an abstract structure. The concrete world must **naturally support** the thematic weight.

A story about leaving oppression works as an ethnic secession because nations actually do this, with all the complexity, fear, and hope that entails. The metaphor has depth because the vehicle (ethnic group seeking independence) has its own rich history and meaning that resonates with the tenor (anyone seeking freedom from oppression).

Choose instantiations where the concrete world adds to the meaning, not just illustrates it.
